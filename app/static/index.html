The architecture gap

Per payment checks evaluate row by row. Fraud rings operate network by network.

When an organized fraud ring distributes funds across multiple accounts, each transaction appears small, ordinary, and clean. Single row checks pass them every time. When transactions are scored as a graph, circular laundering loops, mule consolidation funnels, and shared device signatures become visible.

Traditional checkout checks

Scores transactions in isolation

Evaluates single amounts, card BINs, and email age. Misses multi hop mule consolidation, layering loops, and account farms operating across shared hardware.

RingBreaker graph intelligence

Scores accounts by graph neighborhood

Aggregates multi hop payment flows and shared device tags using GraphSAGE. Authorizes payment first, scores risk during transit, and captures or voids based on graph topology.

Core principle

Single payment checks evaluate one row at a time. RingBreaker evaluates the entire network before money moves.

Measured outcomes

Engineered for risk analysts and high volume merchants

Intercept coordinated multi account transfers while keeping automated false declines under control.

Outcome 01

Early voiding before capture

By keeping orders in an authorized state during scoring, RingBreaker voids fraudulent authorizations before settlement capture occurs.

Outcome 02

Calibrated review holds

Borderline scores hold payments uncaptured rather than declining buyers immediately. Risk teams make final determinations with factual evidence.

Outcome 03

Grounded graph explanations

The AI investigator summarizes transaction clusters citing computed graph facts verified against actual transaction histories.

Execution lifecycle

Three steps from checkout authorization to settlement

1

Authorize order

Checkout creates an order with intent AUTHORIZE. Buyer funds are held but not yet captured by the merchant account.

2

Graph neural scoring

The transaction edge connects to the live graph. GraphSAGE message passing rescores sender and receiver across two hop neighborhoods.

3

Route response tier

Low risk captures automatically. High risk voids immediately. Medium risk routes to the analyst review queue with cluster evidence.

Transparent disclosure

Limitations and honest trade offs

Machine learning risk scoring involves measured trade offs between fraud interception and review burden.

Does RingBreaker have false positives?

Yes. Legitimate behavior such as roommates splitting rent or small business coworkers sharing an office device can resemble multi account clusters. To protect legitimate buyers, borderline transactions route to human review rather than automatic cancellation.

Can fraud rings evade detection?

Yes. If an organized group introduces long delays between transfers (weeks or months) or avoids shared devices, graph context builds slowly. The tunable operating threshold slider allows merchants to increase recall sensitivity at the cost of higher review volume.

What data was the model trained on?

The models were trained and evaluated on a synthetic financial graph with injected ring topologies and hard negatives (roommates, shared kiosks, payroll bursts). Real world deployment requires training on labeled merchant historical ledgers.

Are device fingerprints and IP identifiers real?

No. The PayPal developer sandbox does not provide real device hardware telemetry. Device IDs are simulated metadata attached by the checkout simulator for evaluation.

Why does the GNN beat the tabular baseline?

Across 5 seed evaluations on held out rings, GraphSAGE achieved 0.9882 PR-AUC compared to 0.9097 for a strong XGBoost baseline with 28 engineered graph features. The GNN captures multi hop directed flow patterns and cycle topology directly, whereas tabular aggregations only summarize local neighborhood statistics.

Is real money involved?

No. This project is strictly a prototype for the PayPal AI Hackathon running against the developer sandbox environment.
